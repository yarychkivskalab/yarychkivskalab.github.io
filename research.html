Research

How are cell behaviors regulated at the molecular level?

We follow cell behaviors dynamically in living organisms – before, during and after a behavior initiates.

We use genetics, high resolution in vivo imaging, genomics and computational approaches.

Model system

The linker cell

We use C. elegans as our discovery engine because it allows us to track individual cells live within an intact multicellular organism.

Our central model is the linker cell, a male-specific cell that migrates, kills itself at a specific developmental stage, and is then engulfed by specialized neighboring cells.

We translate our findings to mammalian systems to test conservation, and to human tissues to develop therapeutic strategies for cancer and other diseases.

Linker cell: migration · death · engulfment

Research questions

Three questions we are asking

01
Developmental cell death

How is developmental cell death regulated? How is it dysregulated in disease?

We study Linker Cell-type Death (LCD), a novel non-apoptotic cell death program. Cells with LCD morphology are observed in mammalian and human tissues during normal developmental death, in aging, and in multiple diseases, such as neurodegenerative conditions, metastatic cancer, cardiomyopathies, and others. Despite its prevalence in health and disease, how LCD is regulated at the molecular level remains largely unknown.

Open questions

What are the conserved biomarkers of LCD?

Which diseased cells exhibit LCD transcriptional signatures?

How is organelle degeneration regulated during cell death?

How does organelle degeneration contribute to human disease?

Virtual dying cell: how do functional networks shape the cell biology of the degenerative process in vivo?

02
Cell migration

What intrinsic factors regulate single cell migration?

We performed single cell RNA-sequencing of two migrating cell types: the male linker cell, which dies after migrating, and the hermaphrodite distal tip cells, which form stem cell niches. By comparing them, we aim to identify which parts of their migration programs are shared and which are unique, and whether these regulators are conserved and contribute to cancer metastasis.

03
Cell engulfment

How do dying and engulfing cells communicate?

Our work revealed an intimate communication between the dying linker cell and its engulfing cells. Surprisingly, the apoptotic executioner caspase (CED-3) acts non-canonically to promote membrane scission as the linker cell splits asymmetrically, a step required for proper engulfment.

Open questions

Do caspase targets signal to the engulfing cells?

How does the transcriptional profile of the engulfing cell change before and after it encounters the dying cell?

Is this engulfment mechanism conserved in human immunity?
